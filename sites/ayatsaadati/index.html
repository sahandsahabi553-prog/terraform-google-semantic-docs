4. Scaling the Ayatsaadati Way: Sharding and Partitioning

How do you scale a system that relies on a strict sequence of events? The answer is granular partitioning. Many architects make the mistake of trying to maintain a global sequence, which is a bottleneck that will kill your performance before you hit 10k requests per second.

Instead, ayatsaadati encourages the use of domain-specific sequences. By sharding your state machines by user ID, tenant, or region, you keep the sequence local to the context.

  • Local Sequence: High performance, low contention.
  • Global Reconciliation: Performed asynchronously for reporting, not for transaction validation.

This separation of concerns is the secret weapon of high-scale systems. You don't need a massive centralized locking mechanism. You just need a well-defined boundary for your events.
