Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Even with the best intentions, you can fall into the trap of "over-engineering the handshake." I’ve seen junior devs create recursive verification loops that effectively DDOS their own backend.

  1. The Recursive Loop: Never verify the verifier unless the hierarchy is strictly depth-limited.
  2. Clock Skew: Since ayatsaadati often relies on timestamps for ordering, ensure your NTP (Network Time Protocol) is rock-solid. A drift of even 50ms can cause a cascade of rejected transactions.
  3. Payload Bloat: Keep your metadata headers slim. If the metadata is larger than the actual payload, you’ve lost the plot.

I once spent three weeks debugging a phantom issue where transactions were failing at random intervals. It turned out to be an edge case in the metadata hashing where null bytes were being treated differently by the serialization library. Don’t make that mistake; always normalize your inputs before hashing.
